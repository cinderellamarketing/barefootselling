4 How your Capsule fits together▶

There are three kinds of record, each with its own job.

  • Contacts are your clients: the business, and the people you deal with there. This is where you see who they are, where they came from and who in the team handles them.
  • Policies sit on the Policies board, one card per policy. Capsule calls these projects. A policy's expiry date is the project's expected close date, and the renewal reminders count back from it.
  • Deals are the work: new business, a renewal or a mid-term adjustment. Capsule calls these opportunities, and they move through the Insurance pipeline.

So a client with four policies has one contact, four cards on the Policies board, and a deal each time there's work to do on one of them.

6 Your Insurance pipeline▶

Every deal moves through the same steps, whether it's new business, a renewal or a mid-term adjustment.

WonAccepted, with the policy documents in

A deal that sits in the same stage for 7 days shows a red line, so nothing gets forgotten.

Each deal has a Work type: New business, Renewal or MTA. That's what gives Mike a count of each kind being worked on at any one time. A deal's value is Mike's income from it, and Charlotte updates it from the commission report.

When a referral partner brought the deal in, add the Partner introduction tag to it. That shows an Introducer payment field, so the partner payments can be worked out each month. It goes Pending when the deal is done and the client hasn't paid yet, Owed once it's on your commission report, and Paid once the partner has been paid. A field for the partner's name is added once Mike sends the list of partners.

When you mark a deal as lost, you can choose why: Price, Stayed with existing broker, Moved to another broker, No response, Business closed or sold, or Not something we cover.

5 Renewals and follow-ups you don't have to remember▶

On the call you mentioned a referral that came in that day, for a conversation in February about a renewal in April. At the moment that becomes a diary note you can't see from here.

In Capsule it becomes a dated task on that person's record, with the notes from the first conversation attached. When February comes, it appears in your calendar with the context beside it. Anything missed shows as overdue until it is dealt with.

For existing clients, a renewal checklist runs on each client record: a half-year review, a pre-renewal call and the renewal itself. With retention at 96 to 98%, renewals don't need to sit in a pipeline. The tasks do the job.

6 What goes in the pipeline, and what it's worth▶

New business only. Renewals stay on the client record as tasks, so the insurance pipeline holds new business and what you see there is genuinely new income.

Income, not premium. Each opportunity carries one value. You record the income you expect from it, for example £250 on a £1,000 policy, and refine it as the deal moves through the stages. Each stage carries a probability, so Capsule also shows a projected value across the whole pipeline.

That matters for your targets. At the moment you hear where you stand against the group's income target about twice a year. With a goal on your home screen, you see it every day. Goals and opportunities need to use the same measure, which is another reason to settle on income.

7 Renewal emails without the manual work▶

The email that tells a client their renewal is coming up and asks them to book a meeting goes out one at a time. Transpond, Capsule's sister email tool, sends it from the same data.

  • Your client list stays in Capsule. Transpond is only the tool that sends
  • You filter in Capsule, for example every client renewing in December, and send to that group
  • Once it is set up, the renewal email can be automated rather than sent one by one

You said the system is starting to creak under the number of referrals and the size of the book. Automating the routine emails is how the service stays where it is as both grow.
